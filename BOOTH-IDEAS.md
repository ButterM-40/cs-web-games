# Booth Ideas

## Draw people in

- Put Firewall Frenzy on the biggest screen, facing the walkway.
- Run a daily challenge: "Beat 1,500 on Binary Blitz and win a sticker."
- Set up two laptops for head-to-head Sort Showdown or Binary Blitz.
- Give away prizes at the top of each hour so people come back: stickers, candy, or a small gadget.
- Ask a question they can answer while walking by: "Can you beat a computer at sorting?"

## Connect the game to the department

After each game, ask one question and point to a course. Replace these with your real course names.

| After playing | Say | Point to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Binary Blitz | "Want to know how a CPU uses these bits?" | Computer organization |
| Firewall Frenzy | "Would you have caught that fake website?" | Cybersecurity |
| Bug Hunt | "You just debugged Python. Want to write some?" | Intro programming |
| Sort Showdown | "Why did Quicksort win?" | Data structures and algorithms |
| Robot Commander | "You just wrote a function!" | Intro programming, robotics |

Put a QR code at the booth linking to the department's program page or interest form.

## Activities without a screen

- **Human sorting network:** chalk or tape a network on the floor. Six people holding numbers walk it and come out sorted.
- **Binary bracelets:** students spell their initials in binary with two colors of beads.
- **Caesar cipher wheels:** a paper wheel and a secret message that unlocks a prize.
- **Binary card trick:** "guess the number you're thinking of" using five cards.
- **Program a human:** one student gives exact instructions for making a sandwich while another follows them literally.

## Other games to add

- **Logic gate puzzles:** toggle inputs to light the output.
- **Password cracker meter:** see how long different patterns would take to crack, without typing a real password.
- **Human or AI:** guess which poem or image was made by a person.
- **Train a classifier:** sort examples into two groups and watch a small model learn.
- **Pixel art in hex:** paint with color codes to see how images are stored.
